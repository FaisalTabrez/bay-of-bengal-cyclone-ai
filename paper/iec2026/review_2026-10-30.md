# Critical review of the IEC 2026 paper draft

Review date: 30 October 2026

Manuscript: **Machine Learning for Cyclone Prediction in the Bay of Bengal**

## Assessment and scope

**Recommendation: major revision to research framing and reproducibility before an empirical research submission.** The current document is a useful protocol draft. Its scientific effectiveness cannot yet be assessed because model results and outstanding tests are intentionally on hold. Their absence is an agreed drafting constraint, not an undisclosed failure or a reason to invent preliminary results.

This is a fresh critical review by the assistant that helped prepare the draft. It is not an external peer review or a second human review. Claims were checked against the current manuscript, selected implementation files, project data documentation, the live IEC call and primary literature. No training, calibration, model inference or final-test decoding was performed. No manuscript was changed during this review. The literature check is targeted, not systematic; no plagiarism-detection service was used.

## Major findings

### R1 The scientific contribution is not yet established

**Location:** Introduction, especially the related-work paragraph and final paragraph; Abstract and Conclusion.

The manuscript lists sensible engineering choices but does not state a focused question whose answer would constitute the paper's contribution. Combining known residual networks, missingness masks and Gaussian calibration is not, by itself, a demonstrated research advance. The paper correctly avoids architectural-novelty claims, but it still needs a positive account of what is learned from this particular regional experiment.

The literature context is too narrow: only one cyclone modelling article and one general tabular-learning article support the modelling rationale. Regional intensity estimation and learned cyclone-track uncertainty have already been studied. A 2024 NIO study explicitly compares chronological independent testing with a stratified split and discusses best-track intensity resolution; it is directly relevant to this manuscript's evaluation argument. A 2025 study predicts bivariate Gaussian track uncertainty with a neural network. These are related settings, not interchangeable benchmarks. [Mawatwal and Das, 2024](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s12524-024-01929-8), [Fernandez et al., 2025, author preprint linked to the published article](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.09840).

**Revision:** state one primary research question and distinguish the study from prior work by input type, forecast target, region, lead time, missing-label treatment and evaluation design. A defensible question is whether compact residual models and a frozen-mean uncertainty workflow provide useful skill relative to declared simple references under chronological storm-level evaluation. Establish the answer later using the held results. Do not claim a first application without a substantially broader search.

### R2 The data and model description is not independently reproducible from the manuscript

**Location:** Sections 2 to 5; Data and code availability.

The text gives 39 predictor groups, architecture widths and several training settings, but not the exact feature contract. An independent reader cannot reconstruct units, ERA5 pressure levels, source sampling/interpolation, SST date alignment, geographic eligibility, or the input/target pairing rules from this manuscript alone. Several details exist in repository documentation or the package, but the paper does not identify a frozen supplement that supplies them. A link to a changing repository does not fix this.

The Methods also omits details that affect replication, including the recurrent current-feature skip connection, its head widths, exact residual scaling and the minimum leaf size/L2 regularisation of the tree control. Compact equations for the residual target, masked task loss and composite score would remove ambiguity more efficiently than further procedural prose.

**Revision:** provide a versioned supplement with all feature names, units, transformations, time support, source-extraction definitions, precise model/configuration files and package/code hashes. State that reproducibility is from the supplied derived package until raw-source construction can also be reproduced. This is a documentation correction; it does not require running the held experiments.

### R3 The effective intensity populations are hidden by the total row counts

**Location:** Table 1 and Sections 2, 3 and 5.

Table 1 lists 4560 Bay training pairs but does not show how many have wind or pressure targets. Project documentation reports only 2334 observed wind targets and 2267 observed pressure targets, approximately 51.2% and 49.7% of those pairs. The distinction matters: the track, wind and pressure models do not have the same labelled population. Missingness indicators preserve information but cannot establish that missing labels are representative of all storms.

**Revision:** add observed-target and persistence-eligible counts by partition, or put a compact missingness table in the supplement. Explain whether missingness differs by era, reporting agency or intensity where the available records permit that assessment. Limit intensity conclusions to the evaluated labelled population. Do not imply that masking alone resolves selection bias. The underlying count source is [the model design](../../docs/model-design.md), rather than a new result computed in this review.

### R4 The uncertainty evaluation omits its declared reference distributions

**Location:** Sections 4 and 5.

NLL, CRPS, energy score and coverage are named, but the manuscript does not say which probabilistic reference forecasts make them interpretable as comparative evidence. Coverage near a nominal level can be obtained using broad intervals; it does not demonstrate useful uncertainty estimation on its own.

The current final-evaluation configuration already declares a training-RMS diagonal Gaussian and the frozen uncalibrated Gaussian as references. The scoring implementation evaluates them around the same forecast means and computes paired score differences. These controls are therefore a manuscript omission, not proof that they are missing from the software. Sources: [configuration](../../modelling/configs/final_evaluation_v1.json), [scoring implementation](../../modelling/final_scores.py).

**Revision:** describe those existing references, their fitted populations and the paired comparisons, then report the held outcomes later. Distinguish improvement of the complete calibrated workflow from evidence specifically attributable to input-dependent spread or track correlation. The current controls do not isolate every component. Do not change a frozen model or reuse reserved calibration labels to add an unplanned comparison during this drafting task.

### R5 The draft is already close to the full-paper word limit

**Location:** Whole manuscript; `draft_checks.json` and `build_draft.py`.

The saved counter reports 2559 words, but an independent extraction of Word body paragraphs and table cells gives **2750 word-like tokens** under the same general counting approach. The saved number excludes material such as author text, notation, table cells and captions. Neither number is a publisher-certified count, and text embedded in the figure is not included in the extraction. The paper is still below 3000 on this check, but there is limited space for the held results and a stronger related-work account. It should not simply acquire several additional result sections without cuts. The journal's current limit is 3000 words. [Series B instructions](https://link.springer.com/journal/40031/submission-guidelines).

**Revision:** consolidate repeated hold/claim-boundary statements, move implementation housekeeping and detailed settings into the supplement, and reserve space for the actual findings. Update the count procedure to include all manuscript text that the eventual submission rules require.

## Submission and editorial findings

### R6 IEC and journal abstract requirements should be treated separately

The extended abstract is approximately 634 words and meets the live IEC word limit. However, IEC asks for a narrative including key results and conclusions. The current status paragraph explains that those results are pending. The call also invites concepts and developmental ideas, so that status does not establish automatic ineligibility; it makes a concept/protocol framing more defensible than presenting the abstract as a completed empirical study. Acceptance cannot be inferred. [IEC call](https://41iec.org/papers).

The paper synopsis is 135 words. That meets the IEC-linked generic guidance's maximum of 150, while the provisional Series B journal instructions request 150 to 250. The Word draft follows the linked generic superscript-citation instruction; the Springer copy follows the journal's bracketed-number style. These differences are disclosed in the preparation notes but prevent treating the two outputs as universally identical submission formats. An exactly 150-word synopsis could satisfy both stated length ranges, subject to the journal actually selected. [Series B abstract and reference instructions](https://link.springer.com/journal/40031/submission-guidelines).

### R7 Some text belongs in preparation notes rather than the eventual paper

Sentences such as 'Manuscript preparation neither retrains models nor opens the final test', 'must be traceable in the final supplement' and repeated instructions to complete sections describe document preparation. They make the paper read like a project handoff. Preserve the explicit results placeholder while drafting, but replace these instructions with statements of the actual study protocol before submission. The full-paper abstract also mixes completed-study present tense with an ongoing-work status; align the tense with the eventual submission type.

Funding, competing interests, author contributions and availability statements remain deliberately unresolved. Confirm them with the authors rather than assuming an absence of funding or conflicts. If Series B becomes the actual journal destination, check its separate title-page declaration placement and AI-assistance disclosure instructions. No missing third-author email or ORCID was treated as an automatic rejection condition in this review.

## Strengths that should be retained

- The paper distinguishes retrospective prediction from operational warning capability and identifies unresolved source timing.
- Cyclones remain together in chronological roles; selection and calibration diagnostics are not presented as independent final-test evidence.
- Missing future targets are not imputed, and observed future labels are retained when current intensity is absent.
- Baseline comparisons use matched denominators; all-observed-label reporting is planned separately.
- Track coordinates, covariance scaling and the distinction between a single Gaussian and a mixture are described consistently with the inspected implementation.
- The broader NIO training set is correctly identified as containing the Bay training set.
- Existing citations support the narrow statements inspected; this review did not identify a fabricated citation. That is not a comprehensive reference or plagiarism clearance.

## Recommended revision order

1. Tighten the research question and expand the focused related-work discussion.
2. Complete the feature/provenance supplement and expose the observed-label populations.
3. Describe the probabilistic controls already declared in the current implementation.
4. Remove repeated preparation instructions and recover space under the word limit.
5. Keep results and dependent conclusions on hold; when the project is complete, insert only verified findings with their actual selection/calibration/test roles.
6. Finalise the abstract, declarations and exact destination-specific formatting for the submission being made.

The current protocol has useful safeguards, but those safeguards are not yet a demonstrated scientific result. The strongest eventual paper would make a focused, supported regional comparison and explain what the evidence establishes, including negative or inconclusive outcomes.
